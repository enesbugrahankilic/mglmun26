Who we are

A conference built on preparation, not performance.

MGLMUN’26 brings students together to practise diplomacy the way it is really done: reading the files, writing position papers, building coalitions and turning debate into agreed text.

3Conference days

What happens in committee

Opening speeches, moderated and unmoderated caucuses, working papers and a final draft that the committee votes on.

What you prepare

Your committee’s study guide and a one-to-two page position paper for your delegation.

What you take home

Real negotiation experience, a stronger English speaking voice and a certificate of participation.

Economic · General

WTO

World Trade Organization

Agenda item

The Impact of the Escalating U.S.–China Strategic Rivalry on Global Trade Routes

The rivalry between the world’s two largest economies is reshaping how goods move across the globe. Export controls on semiconductors, mineral licensing and repeated route disruptions in the Red Sea and Panama have turned trade policy into security policy.

Delegates will negotiate how WTO members can protect supply chains, keep trade routes open and prevent the global economy from splitting into rival blocs, all within the rules of the multilateral trading system.

Key questions for delegates

  • Maritime chokepoints and route diversification
  • Critical minerals and semiconductor supply chains
  • Tariffs, export controls and Article XXI
  • Support for developing and transit states
  • Dispute settlement reform
TypeGeneral Assembly-style, consensus first
Delegations24 member states
OutcomeDraft Ministerial Decision

Social · General

UN Women

UN Entity for Gender Equality and the Empowerment of Women

Agenda item

Women’s Rights Violations in the Gaza Strip

Armed conflict affects women and girls in specific ways, from displacement and the loss of maternal and reproductive health care to food insecurity and gender-based violence.

Delegates will examine the situation of women and girls in the Gaza Strip under international humanitarian and human rights law, and work towards concrete measures on protection, humanitarian access, accountability and women’s participation in recovery.

Key questions for delegates

  • Humanitarian access and health care for women and girls
  • Protection from gender-based violence
  • Documentation and accountability mechanisms
  • Women’s participation in relief and recovery

Historical · Crisis

Petrograd Soviet

Petrograd Soviet of Workers’ and Soldiers’ Deputies

Agenda item

Russian Civil War

The Great War destroyed the already shaky Russia, and the failure of the Provisional Government led to the Russian Civil War. From the Dnieper to the Amur, the Motherland burns. Workers desire liberation and peasants require land to farm.

In this committee delegates will discuss how to establish the Soviet Union and win the Russian Civil War: from building the Red Army to land redistribution, from enlarging the Communist Party to defeating the counter-revolutionaries.

Key questions for delegates

  • Organising the Red Army
  • Land redistribution and the peasantry
  • Growing the Communist Party
  • Responding to crisis updates in real time

FAQ

Questions before you apply

I have never been to a MUN. Can I apply?

Yes. Write “None” under previous experience. The study guides explain the agenda, and chairs help first-time delegates during the sessions.

How are countries allocated?

The academic team allocates countries and roles after reviewing applications, using your three committee preferences and your motivation letter.

What is a position paper?

A one-to-two page document setting out your delegation’s view on the agenda item and the solutions it supports. The submission deadline will be announced by the Secretariat.

What language is used?

All committees work in English.

How is my personal data used?

Your data is processed only to organise MGLMUN’26, under Law No. 6698 (KVKK). Photo and video consent and stakeholder sharing are optional.
